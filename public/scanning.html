OPT OUT // no justification required

If you do not want us touching your address space, say so and we stop. You do not have to explain why, and we will not ask you to justify it. Exclusions are applied when we receive them — not when we finish reviewing them — and they do not expire.

Open a removal request

Prefer not to use GitHub? Email works and carries exactly the same weight — see the contact on the maintainer's profile. Needing an account in order to be left alone would not be an opt-out.

What we send

Every request is a read-only GET against a version, health, or listing endpoint. We are checking whether a service answers an unauthenticated request. We never send one to prove what that would allow.

We never submit a job, pull a model, send a prompt, start a kernel, read a file, or attempt to authenticate. We do not follow redirects, so we cannot be bounced onto a different host. We do not scan ports: each service is only ever contacted on its own known port.

Identify us by the User-Agent LeakyCompute-SafeProbe or LeakyCompute-MultiLane.

How your host came to our attention

We do not find hosts by scanning. Every address we contact either already appeared in a public index such as Shodan or Censys, or its owner asked us to check it. Our traffic never reveals anything that was not already published by someone else.

This also means opting out here does not remove you from those indexes. Shodan and Censys are separate services with their own removal processes. And if a host is genuinely exposed, opting out of our measurement does not close it — the people who are not asking permission will still find it.

What we keep

Per host: address, port, service, version string, first and last seen, and geo/ASN metadata. We do not retain model lists, job records, or page contents. Records are deleted 180 days after we last saw the host answer. Raw addresses are never published — public figures are aggregate counts by country, ASN, and stack.

If you are exposed

The fastest fixes, in order: bind the service to 127.0.0.1 instead of 0.0.0.0; restrict the port at your host firewall and cloud security group; put an authenticating reverse proxy in front if you need remote access. The self-check on the home page reports the specific remediation for whatever it finds, and runs against your own connecting IP with no input from you.

Rules we hold ourselves to

The project's invariants are written down and versioned, so this page can be checked against the code rather than taken on trust: SECURITY.md. If you can make this checker probe something it should not, that is a security issue in our software and we want to hear about it — open a private advisory on the repository.
